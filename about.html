About

Approvals that stay attached to what was signed.

Attest is a Confluence Cloud app for controlled-document approvals. Here's what it's for, who runs it and where it stands.

What Attest is for

Attest is for teams whose documents have to be approved, stay approved and show the evidence: quality manuals, SOPs, policies and controlled procedures. Its job is to keep an approval attached to the exact version someone signed, and to end that approval when the version changes or its review date passes.

How it's built

Attest is built on Atlassian Forge and runs on Atlassian's infrastructure. It has no servers, no database and no outside network calls of its own. One module decides which state changes are legal, and the panel and the server both use the same rules for who may make them. The audit code can only add entries. The state machine, quorum arithmetic, date handling, CSV writer and hash chain are covered by 74 unit tests.

Principles

State limits plainly. The app's known limits are published on these pages. A buyer of a document-control product is entitled to know where it stops before installing it.

Make no compliance claims a vendor can't make. Nobody can sell you 21 CFR Part 11 compliance or ISO 13485 certification in an app. Attest makes neither claim. The accurate claim is approval records you can hand to an auditor.

A record should say when it can't be relied on. If a lock didn't apply, the panel says so. If the content no longer matches what was signed, the record says so. If the record can't load, it tells the reader not to rely on it.

Evidence should be checkable without the vendor. The audit export can be verified by a short script that shares no code with the app, using a hash formula published on the Product page.

Who runs it

Attest is built and operated by Tommy Newman, as a sole operator. Tommy writes the code and answers support himself, and the terms of service are with him directly.

Working with one person has a plain trade-off. You deal directly with whoever wrote the code, and you're depending on one person. The terms promise reasonable notice before the app is withdrawn. Your data stays on Atlassian's infrastructure, your page history stays in Confluence, and a space administrator can export the audit trail at any time.
